# Pre-Pump / Pre-Dump Detection for Binance USD-M Perpetual Futures: Evidence Base and System Architecture

## TL;DR
- **The single strongest, best-documented predictive edge for the "coordinated pump" case is not a price/OI indicator at all — it is monitoring the coordination channel itself.** Academic work (Xu & Livshits 2019; Hu et al. 2023; La Morgia et al. 2023) shows Telegram/Discord message monitoring predicts *which* coin will be pumped (AUC >0.9; Hit-Ratio@5 ≈ 53%) and detects a pump within ~25 seconds of onset (F1 94.5%) — but almost all of this literature targets small-cap *spot* microcap pumps, not liquid Binance USD-M perps, so transfer is only partial. For organic macro/leverage-driven moves, the honest answer is that no single indicator has published, durable, net-of-cost predictive power at the "overnight" horizon; the realistic ceiling of a signal-only system is a modest, calibrated precision uplift over base rate on a filtered watchlist, not reliable point prediction.
- **Tiered by evidence quality:** (1) *Empirically supported* — volatility clustering/contraction predicting volatility *expansion* (magnitude, not direction); order-flow/order-book imbalance predicting very-short-horizon (seconds–minutes) direction; BTC→alt lead-lag; P&D coordination-signal detection. (2) *Plausible-mechanistic but weakly validated for prediction* — OI buildup, funding extremes as crowding/contrarian context, liquidation clusters as catalysts, exchange flows. (3) *Folklore with little rigorous support* — most SMC/ICT constructs (order blocks, liquidity sweeps as *predictive* rules), CVD divergence as a standalone timing signal, L2 spoofing/wall reading in a manual system.
- **Architecture recommendation:** Rebuild as a **two-stage confluence + meta-labeling system** — an unsupervised/statistical anomaly layer (cross-sectionally normalized z-scores/percentile ranks) generates candidates; a supervised meta-labeling layer (triple-barrier labels, calibrated probabilities, event-based PR-AUC/precision@k evaluation) filters them. Treat all numeric thresholds as starting heuristics requiring your own calibration, evaluate on *events* not bars, and budget explicitly for a high false-positive rate.

## Key Findings

1. **Order-book/order-flow imbalance is the most rigorously validated micro-signal, but its predictive horizon (seconds to ~1–5 minutes) is largely destroyed by human execution latency.** It belongs in your system as *confirmation context at a level*, not as a manual entry trigger.
2. **Volatility contraction predicting expansion is real and is one of your better foundations — but it forecasts *magnitude, not direction*.** You must pair it with a separate directional signal, and the published edge is modest (directional accuracy ~55–60% in vendor backtests, with asymmetric payoff doing the work).
3. **Funding rate and open interest are best understood as *coincident crowding/positioning* measures, not leading return predictors.** Funding-rate *level* is highly predictable period-to-period, but that is not the same as predicting price. Extreme funding is a legitimate contrarian/fragility context flag, not a timing signal.
4. **The pump-and-dump academic literature is the crown jewel for your "full-market scanner," and it is directly about prediction/early-detection** — but it is built on microcap spot coins and coordination-message monitoring. The market-data-only detectors have inherent latency; the ex-ante *target* predictors lean heavily on social/coordination features.
5. **SMC/ICT concepts lack rigorous empirical validation and their "institutional manipulation" narrative is essentially unfalsifiable.** They can still function as a *level-selection heuristic* (where stops cluster), but should not be coded as validated predictive rules.
6. **The dominant failure mode of any such system is the base-rate/false-positive problem: pre-pump-like conditions occur constantly.** Serious systems handle this with event-based evaluation, precision@k, probability calibration, and per-regime recalibration — not bar-by-bar accuracy.

## Details

### PART A — Evidence base for predictive signals

**A0. Framing note on the two example moves.** A +24% overnight run (8.29→10.32) and a −20.7% short (0.11929→0.09464) are, in magnitude, exactly the profile of small/mid-cap altcoin perp moves that the P&D and volatility-expansion literature describes. Neither can be reverse-engineered into proof that any specific indicator "would have caught it" — that is precisely the survivorship/hindsight trap (see B4). Treat them as *representative targets to be evaluated against a properly held-out event set*, not as validation anchors.

**A1. Open interest (OI) dynamics.** Mechanistically well-motivated: OI measures outstanding leveraged exposure, and the practitioner taxonomy (rising OI + rising price = new longs; rising OI + flat price = fragile two-sided buildup; falling OI during a move = position unwinds) is internally coherent. But this is *plausible-mechanistic*, not empirically validated as a leading return predictor. **Critical data-quality caveat:** a peer-reviewed study (Giagkiozis & Said, *Ledger* Vol. 9, 2024) found that "open interest in Bitcoin perpetual swaps is systematically misquoted by some of the largest derivatives exchanges," with the degree varying by venue — so cross-exchange aggregated OI and OI z-scores are built on partly unreliable inputs. Use *per-symbol Binance-native* OI, compute z-scores on it, and treat "OI buildup at flat price" as a *fragility/context* flag (a crowded book that can move violently) rather than a directional signal. There is a related, more rigorous strand — retail speculative net positioning predicting crypto returns (published in a finance journal) — but that uses COT-style positioning data, not raw OI, and operates at longer horizons.

**A2. Funding rate.** Two distinct facts must be separated. (i) *Funding level is predictable*: a study on Binance/Bybit BTC perps (Inan, "Predictability of Funding Rates," SSRN) found double-autoregressive models beat a no-change benchmark on next-period funding in both error and directional accuracy — but this predicts *funding*, not price, and the authors note predictability is time-varying. (ii) *Funding as a price signal* is coincident, not leading. Practitioner and exchange sources converge on funding being a "sentiment and positioning tool" that "does not predict price direction on its own"; extreme readings flag crowded positioning and elevated squeeze/cascade risk. The commonly cited "elevated" threshold for BTC (>~0.05%/8h) is a heuristic, not a validated constant, and **Binance now uses dynamic per-symbol funding caps**, so a single cross-symbol threshold is invalid — you must compute per-symbol funding percentiles/z-scores. Best use: **contrarian/fragility context** ("who is crowded and paying to stay there"), gated with a catalyst. The funding-arbitrage literature is about *carry*, not directional prediction: per Chen et al., "The Two-Tiered Structure of Cryptocurrency Funding Rate Markets," *Mathematics* (MDPI) 14(2):346, on 35.7M one-minute observations across 26 exchanges/749 symbols, "While 17% of observations exhibit economically significant arbitrage spreads (≥20 basis points), only 40% of top opportunities generate positive returns after transaction costs and spread reversals" — a useful reminder that raw signal frequency ≠ tradeable edge.

**A3. Liquidation clusters/cascades.** The literature treats these as *catalysts and amplifiers*, not clean predictors. Cheng et al. (2021, BitMEX extreme-value study) quantified daily forced-liquidation probabilities (3.51% of longs, 1.89% of shorts) at ~60× average leverage among liquidated positions — establishing that leverage buildup creates a *combustible state*. The reflexive mechanism (a 2–3% spot move triggering 15–30% leverage-driven drawdowns via cascades) is well described by recent events:
- **Oct 10–11, 2025:** $19.37 billion in leveraged positions wiped out across 1.6 million+ traders over 24 hours — "the largest ever liquidation event tracked by crypto-focused data analytics firm CoinGlass" (CNBC). Amberdata recorded "$3.21 billion in positions evaporated in 60 seconds" at 21:15 UTC; per CoinDesk Data, average token declines reached ~47% (surpassing the May 2021 crash at ~41%), and total perpetual-futures open interest contracted 43% to $123 billion (from $217B), led by a 57% drop on Hyperliquid.
- **Nov 21, 2025:** CoinGlass reported 391,000 traders liquidated with total liquidations of $1.91 billion (~$1.86B of it longs) as BTC broke ~$85K to $81,050 and the Fear & Greed Index hit 11.

For your system, liquidation-cluster *heatmaps* (where stops/liq levels pile up) are a plausible "magnet/target" context; the cascade itself is generally too fast for manual execution and is better used to *avoid* being on the wrong side than to enter.

**A4. Cumulative Volume Delta (CVD) and divergence.** CVD (running taker-buy minus taker-sell) is mechanically sound and one of the few tape signals that transfers cleanly from equities to crypto perps. **But there is essentially no rigorous published evidence that CVD *divergence* is a reliable standalone predictive timing signal** — the entire evidence base is practitioner/vendor material, which itself repeatedly warns "divergence is an early warning, not a guaranteed reversal." Two methodological cautions: (i) *aggregated* cross-exchange CVD is fragile because trade-classification and venue coverage differ; prefer per-venue (Binance-native) CVD. (ii) Spot-vs-perp CVD divergence has a coherent mechanistic story (perp-led speculative pressure vs spot conviction) but no rigorous validation. Use CVD as *confirmation of conviction behind a move*, not as a trigger.

**A5. Volume anomalies.** The cleanest empirical result is that **volume predicts *volatility/activity* far more reliably than it predicts *return direction*.** In equities, abnormal trading volume (ATV) does predict returns but the *direction is complex/regime-dependent* (short-run continuation, long-run reversal). In crypto, return-volume causality is unstable and often insignificant (COVID-era Granger tests found no return-volume causality for BTC). And a crypto-specific factor study (the CTREND trend-factor line, *Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis* 2025) found trading volume "insignificant within our sample, demonstrating no return predictability over cryptocurrency returns." **Verdict:** a volume z-score spike is a strong *"something is happening"* flag (high recall for events) but low precision on *direction* — exactly the profile that makes it a good first-stage candidate generator, bad standalone signal. Also weigh wash-trading contamination on low-liquidity symbols.

**A6. Volatility contraction → expansion (Bollinger/ATR squeeze).** This is your **best-supported chart pattern**, grounded in the robust academic finding that crypto volatility exhibits strong clustering and persistence (GARCH β typically 0.7–0.9; α+β near 1), which mechanically implies volatility is forecastable and that low-vol regimes are followed by high-vol regimes. **But:** (i) the rigorous part is *volatility* forecastability; the "squeeze predicts a big move" claim is a corollary, and the "which direction" question is *not* answered by the squeeze. Vendor backtests report squeeze-breakout directional accuracy of only ~55–60%, with positive expectancy coming from asymmetric payoff (winners run further than losers), and John Bollinger himself warned bands are descriptive, not predictive, and flagged the "head fake" false-breakout. **Verdict:** use ATR/BBW percentile-rank compression as a *magnitude/timing prior* ("expansion is due"), always paired with an independent directional input; treat the 55–60% and "bottom 5% band-width" numbers as heuristics to re-derive on your own symbols.

**A7. Order-book imbalance / L2 microstructure.** This is the **most rigorously validated micro-signal in the entire survey**: order-flow imbalance (OFI) has a near-linear, empirically robust relationship with short-horizon price changes (Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov and successors), with slope inversely proportional to depth; deep-learning LOB models (DeepLOB; Kolm/Westray "Deep OFI") confirm ubiquitous short-horizon predictability. **The fatal constraint for your use case:** this predictability lives at **50ms–5min horizons**, decaying fast. One source notes profitability "persists even with small execution delays … bringing the result into the realm of discretionary trading rather than pure HFT" — but "small" means sub-minute, not the seconds-to-minutes-plus of a human reading a Telegram alert. **Verdict:** L2/spoofing/wall dynamics are **not usable as a manual-execution trigger**; they are legitimate *background context* (is the bid being defended/refilled at a level?) and are only actionable if automated. Do not build manual signals on wall-watching.

**A8. Trade-level order flow (aggressive buy/sell imbalance, large-trade detection).** Same family as A7 and, importantly, this is exactly where the P&D detection literature found its edge: La Morgia et al.'s "rush orders" (abnormal growth of aggressive market buy orders) are the single most important feature (Gini importance 0.251) for detecting a pump within 25 seconds. Large-trade / aggressive-imbalance detection therefore has *real, published detection value* — but note it's *detection at onset*, not *ex-ante prediction*, and again the manual-latency problem bites for the fastest moves. For an "overnight" horizon it is more forgiving.

**A9. SMC/ICT concepts (order blocks, liquidity sweeps, stop hunts, accumulation/distribution).** **Honest assessment: little to no rigorous empirical validation, and the core "institutional manipulation" narrative is unfalsifiable.** Independent critical sources note the manipulation story "lacks empirical support from order flow research" and that failed breakouts/liquidity sweeps are equally explained by "normal market mechanics: thin order books at extremes, momentum cascades." The only "evidence" is self-published community backtests reporting 50–65% win rates under heavy discretionary confluence filtering — non-independent, subjective, and unfalsifiable ("valid displacement," "clean sweep" are judgment calls that resist systematic backtesting; even one ICT-favorable source concedes "roughly half of those work and half fake out"). **Salvageable core:** the *location* logic is sound and overlaps with real microstructure — stops cluster above swing highs / below swing lows, and thin books at extremes produce sweep-and-reverse patterns. Use SMC as a **heuristic for *where* liquidity/stops sit** (feeding your liquidation-cluster map), not as a validated predictive rule set.

**A10. On-chain signals (exchange flows, whale movements, stablecoin flows).** Mechanistically reasonable and there is *some* empirical support at the aggregate BTC level: de Haan et al., "Forecasting Bitcoin volatility spikes from whale transactions and CryptoQuant data using Synthesizer Transformer models" (arXiv:2211.08281), found net BTC exchange flow (abs(BTCplus−BTCminus)) "has a Pearson correlation of 0.47 with daily BTC price volatility" across 330 volatility spikes — again, *volatility*, not direction. **Two decisive limitations for your use case:** (i) most rigorous work is BTC/ETH; coverage and signal quality for the *altcoin perps* where your example moves live is thin, noisy, and often unavailable in real time; (ii) individual whale-alert transactions have "abysmal" signal-to-noise — only *sustained aggregate directional flow* carries information, which is a slow signal poorly matched to an overnight scanner. **Verdict:** useful as a slow BTC/ETH-level *regime/context* input; largely impractical as a per-altcoin pre-move trigger.

**A11. Cross-asset / lead-lag.** Reasonably supported and directly useful. BTC Granger-causes major alts (ETH, BNB, ADA) at daily lags; there is a documented "seesaw"/cross-predictability structure. Per Jia et al., "A seesaw effect in the cryptocurrency market," *Pacific-Basin Finance Journal* (2023), "large coins lead small coins but not" vice versa, driven by a "flight-to-hot-coins" effect (with the large coins *negatively* predicting next-period small-coin returns — note the sign is non-obvious and regime-dependent); see also Guo, Sang, Tu & Wang, "Cross-cryptocurrency return predictability," *Journal of Economic Dynamics & Control* 163:104863 (2024). BTC/ETH-vs-alt lead-lag and sector rotation are legitimate context features, but the relationships are unstable over time (crypto lead-lag "shifts rapidly") and must be re-estimated per regime. **Verdict:** a solid *conditioning/context* layer (don't fight BTC), not a precise timing engine.

**A12. Social/sentiment/listing announcements and the P&D detection literature.** This is the richest *prediction-oriented* literature and the most relevant to your full-market scanner:
- **Xu & Livshits (2019, USENIX Security), "The Anatomy of a Cryptocurrency Pump-and-Dump Scheme"** — random-forest model predicting *which coin* gets pumped, ex ante, with **AUC >0.9 in- and out-of-sample**; features were market cap, volume, and especially *return* features ("return features are generally more important than volume"), coin age/rating, and prior-pump count; a hypothetical strategy "can generate a return as high as 60% on small retail investments within a span of two and half months." Sample: 412 P&D events; the prediction set was 47,487 observations with only 133 pumped (0.3% — extreme imbalance). Comparison GLM overfit (in-sample AUC 0.92, out-of-sample 0.86).
- **Hu et al. (2023, ACM SIGMOD/PACMMOD), "Sequence-Based Target Coin Prediction"** — predicts the target coin one hour before the pump: **AUC 0.943, Hit-Ratio@3 43.0%, Hit-Ratio@5 53.2%**, vs a random baseline HR@5 of just 1.6% (709 events, 278 coins, 18 exchanges). Finding: "coins with middle-cap value and high discussion degree in social media and forums are more likely to be targeted."
- **La Morgia et al. (2023, ACM TOIT, "The Doge of Wall Street")** — real-time *detection* via "rush orders" (abnormal growth of aggressive market buy orders): **F1 94.5%, precision 98.2%, recall 91.2% at 25-second chunks**, vs the prior hourly-candlestick baseline (Kamps et al.) at F1 62.7% with ~30-min expected latency (317 P&Ds; 5-fold CV; StdRushOrders is the top feature at Gini importance 0.251).
- **Victor & Hagemann (2019, IEEE ICDMW), "Quantification and Detection"** — XGBoost detection at **sensitivity 85%, specificity 99%, AUC 0.995** on 149 confirmed Binance-pair schemes (features: volume bars, price-change rates, time differences); identified 612 pump-like events among 172 coins, concentrated in low-cap coins.
- **Real-time NLP pipeline (Bolz et al., arXiv:2412.18848, 2024)** — Z-score model identified the pumped coin in the top-5 in **55.81% (24/43) of Poloniex events**, top-10 in 74.42%.
- **PumpSense (Mahrous & Di Pietro, arXiv:2605.09431, 2026)** — message-level detection on 280,000+ Telegram posts / 2,246 pump announcements: LightGBM F1 0.79 (29% false positives, 12% missed pumps), transformer BGE-M3 F1 0.83; LLM ticker extraction accuracy 0.91. Notes that 10 groups drive ~70% of pump events.
- **Listing announcements:** event studies find significant abnormal returns around crypto listing/delisting/regulatory events, with *negative* events showing larger effects, and some evidence (Jo et al. 2020) that abnormal returns appear up to three days *before* announcements (information leakage). The Binance-listing "pop" is real but fast and largely spot-side.

**Two critical transfer caveats for Part A12:** (i) This literature is overwhelmingly about **small-cap spot coins on second-tier exchanges** (Poloniex, Yobit, Cryptopia, Binance spot pairs) — coordinated microcap pumps. Liquid **Binance USD-M perps** are much harder to pump/dump coordinatedly, so the *coordination-signal* edge is diluted for large caps but may still apply to newly-listed / low-float perps. (ii) The best *ex-ante target* predictors (Xu, Hu) lean on **coordination/social features**; the market-data-only detectors are fast but essentially *coincident* (they fire as the pump begins). For a signal-only system with human latency, the actionable insight is: **monitor coordination channels and newly-listed low-float symbols, and use market-data anomaly detection as corroboration, accepting it will mostly be coincident.**

### PART B — Architectural and methodological paradigms

**B1. Detector paradigm trade-offs.**
- *Rule-based / confluence-gate (N-of-M):* transparent, no training data needed, easy to reason about — this is likely what "Hunt" was. Weakness: thresholds are hand-set and un-calibrated, confluence of correlated signals overstates confidence, and it has no principled false-positive control. Good as a *candidate generator*, poor as a final arbiter.
- *Statistical/unsupervised anomaly detection:* well-suited to the "pre-pump-like conditions" problem because you don't need labeled pumps and it naturally normalizes per-symbol (the P&D EWMA/threshold and Z-score papers use exactly this). Weakness: flags *all* anomalies, not just tradeable ones — low precision.
- *Supervised ML (classification of pre-move states):* highest ceiling (Xu/Hu/La Morgia all supervised), but needs labels, is exposed to lookahead/overfitting, and requires the imbalanced-data discipline below.
- *Recommended hybrid:* **unsupervised anomaly layer → supervised meta-labeling filter.** Stage 1 (cheap, high-recall) narrows thousands of symbol-bars to a candidate watchlist; Stage 2 (supervised, calibrated) estimates P(tradeable move | candidate) and controls precision. This mirrors both López de Prado's meta-labeling and the two-stream P&D pipelines.

**B2. Feature engineering & cross-symbol normalization.** The central requirement (and a likely reason a naive system fails) is **normalization across heterogeneous symbols**: a raw volume or OI number is meaningless across a $30B and a $30M coin. Use **per-symbol rolling z-scores and percentile ranks** and **volatility-normalization** (divide moves/targets by ATR or realized vol) so a "3σ volume spike" or "95th-percentile OI" means the same thing on every symbol. The P&D thresholding literature's key lesson is that *per-token* thresholds are what separate genuine anomalies from noise on low-liquidity coins.

**B3. Base-rate / false-positive problem & evaluation.** Pre-pump-like conditions are common, true pre-pumps are rare (Xu's 0.3% base rate is illustrative). Consequences: (i) **use PR-AUC / average precision and precision@k, not accuracy or ROC-AUC** — ROC-AUC is misleadingly optimistic under extreme imbalance (a model can show ROC-AUC 0.98 with PR-AUC 0.10). (ii) **Evaluate on *events*, not bars** — bar-by-bar accuracy is dominated by true negatives and tells you nothing; score whether each *move event* was flagged in a preceding window (the P&D papers evaluate per-event, e.g., "flag from public announcement up to 60 min beforehand"). (iii) Report **FPR at fixed recall** and precision@k (k = your realistic daily alert budget) so the metric matches how a human consumes alerts. (iv) Expect and design for high FPR — even the strong PumpSense model shows 29% false positives.

**B4. Avoiding survivorship / lookahead / data-snooping bias.** Because your training signal is "moves that already happened," you are acutely exposed:
- *Survivorship:* include **delisted/dead symbols** and the full historical Binance USD-M universe as it existed at each date (point-in-time listings), or you'll overstate edge.
- *Lookahead:* every feature must use only data available at decision time; funding, OI, and "aggregated" feeds are often *revised/backfilled* — use point-in-time snapshots.
- *Data-snooping / multiple testing:* trying many thresholds/features inflates false discoveries. Use **deflated Sharpe ratio / multiple-testing corrections** (Bailey & López de Prado), purged & embargoed cross-validation (to prevent label leakage across overlapping windows), and hold out a final untouched event set. The crypto cross-predictability literature explicitly invoked Harvey's t>3 multiple-hypothesis threshold to guard against p-hacking — adopt the same bar.

**B5. Labeling methodology.** Use the **triple-barrier method (López de Prado):** for each candidate, set an upper (TP), lower (SL), and vertical (time) barrier sized to your overnight horizon and to *volatility* (ATR-scaled, not fixed %), label by which is hit first. Then **meta-label**: a primary model/rule proposes side, a secondary model learns P(primary is correct) and thus *bet size / whether to alert at all* — this is precisely the "filter out bad signals" job your scanner needs. Consider trend-scanning labels if fixed horizons feel arbitrary. Independent capstone work (Hudson & Thames) confirms event-based sampling + triple-barrier + meta-labeling improves strategy performance versus fixed-horizon labeling.

**B6. Regime dependence & sample size.** Crypto relationships (lead-lag, funding behavior, volatility) are **strongly regime-dependent** and shift fast; a model calibrated in a trending bull regime will misfire in chop. Requirements: (i) tag regimes (BTC trend/vol state, market-wide funding, breadth) and calibrate per regime; (ii) recognize per-regime calibration *fractures your sample* — you need enough *events* per regime for stable estimates (rule of thumb: tens of events minimum per regime/threshold before trusting a precision estimate; the P&D papers work with 149–709 events *total*, which is already tight for per-regime slicing). Prefer robust, low-parameter models over deep nets given the event counts.

**B7. TP/SL methodology & EV framing.** Ground everything in **expected value, not win rate** — the squeeze/breakout evidence (55–60% directional accuracy but positive expectancy via large winners) is the canonical illustration that win-rate alone is insufficient. Concretely:
- **Stops:** ATR-anchored (a multiple of ATR) *and/or* structure-anchored (beyond the swing that invalidates the thesis) — use the *tighter-justified* of the two so the stop sits where the idea is actually wrong, not at an arbitrary %. The commonly cited ATR multiples (1.5–3×) are heuristics to calibrate, not constants.
- **Targets:** reconcile TP with a **movement-magnitude forecast** — this is where the volatility-expansion estimate earns its keep: size the target to the *expected* expansion (ATR/realized-vol projection or measured-move), not a fixed R. Use **partial-TP ladders** (scale out) to convert an uncertain magnitude forecast into realized PnL while leaving a runner.
- **R-multiple vs structure-projected:** compute R from the structure-anchored stop, then require the *structure/vol-projected* target to clear a minimum R (e.g., ≥1.5–2R after costs) as a gate. If the honest magnitude forecast can't clear costs+slippage at the required R, **don't alert** — this is the EV gate that a signal-only system most needs.

**B8. Probability calibration.** A raw model *score* is not a probability. Produce **calibrated probabilities** (Platt/isotonic), verify with **reliability diagrams** and **Brier score**, so that "70%" alerts hit ~70% of the time. This is what makes a signal-only system trustworthy to a human executor and lets you set a principled alert threshold tied to your EV gate.

**B9. Realistic performance ceiling.** Be blunt with yourself: (i) The only durable, published, net-of-cost edges in this space are narrow — cross-sectional factor/trend strategies (CTREND survives costs but is a slow portfolio strategy; the cross-predictability study reports minute-level long-short spreads of only ~1.5–3.3bp), funding carry (a yield strategy, not directional prediction), and coordinated-P&D detection (which is mostly *coincident* on liquid perps). (ii) Most pre-move "detectors" have high false-positive rates by construction. (iii) For liquid Binance USD-M perps specifically, there is **no published evidence of a signal that reliably predicts an overnight directional move with tradeable precision net of costs**. The achievable goal is a **calibrated precision uplift over base rate on a filtered candidate set** — turning a 0.3–1% base rate into, say, a 10–30% precision alert stream at low recall — which, combined with disciplined EV-gated TP/SL, can be positive-expectancy. Anything promising reliable point prediction of moves like your two examples is overfitting or folklore.

## Recommendations

**Stage 0 — Instrument and honestly re-baseline (do this before building signals).**
- Rebuild your historical dataset **point-in-time** over the full Binance USD-M universe *including delisted symbols*. Without this, every backtest is survivorship-inflated.
- Define your target as **events** (e.g., ≥X ATR-normalized move within an N-hour window) and compute the **base rate** per symbol and per regime. This number is your honesty benchmark — any detector must beat it on precision@k.
- Re-evaluate "Hunt" on this event set with PR-AUC and precision@k. You will likely find it was a high-recall, low-precision confluence gate — useful as Stage 1, not as a final signal.

**Stage 1 — Candidate generation (unsupervised/statistical, high recall).**
- Per-symbol, per-bar features, all **z-scored / percentile-ranked and vol-normalized**: volume z-score, ATR/BBW compression percentile, OI z-score (Binance-native only), funding percentile (per-symbol, respecting dynamic caps), realized-vol regime, aggressive-trade imbalance, BTC/ETH lead-lag state.
- Fire a candidate when a *configurable N-of-M* anomaly cluster triggers. Accept that this is noisy — its job is recall.

**Stage 2 — Meta-labeling filter (supervised, calibrated, precision control).**
- Label candidates with the **triple-barrier method** (ATR-scaled barriers, overnight vertical barrier). Train a low-parameter model (gradient-boosted trees) to output **P(tradeable move | candidate)**.
- Use **purged/embargoed CV**, deflated-Sharpe/multiple-testing discipline, and a held-out event set.
- **Calibrate** probabilities (isotonic), check reliability + Brier, and only emit a Telegram alert above a probability threshold tied to your **EV gate** (expected R after costs+slippage ≥ your minimum).

**Stage 3 — Two specialized modules.**
- *Pinned-watchlist intraday module:* richer per-symbol features, CVD/order-flow *confirmation* at pre-identified levels (SMC/liquidation-cluster map used only for *where*, not *whether*), tighter monitoring.
- *Full-market scanner targeting pre-pump/pre-dump:* prioritize **newly-listed / low-float perps** and, if feasible, **coordination-channel monitoring** (this is where the published ex-ante edge actually lives — Xu/Hu features and message NLP). Rank by calibrated probability, alert top-k within your daily budget.

**Stage 4 — TP/SL and delivery.**
- Every alert carries a **structure-or-ATR-anchored stop**, a **vol/measured-move-projected target with a partial-TP ladder**, the **R-multiple**, and the **calibrated probability** — so the human executor sees EV, not just a signal.

**Benchmarks / thresholds that change the plan:**
- If Stage 2 precision@k can't beat the Stage 0 base rate by a meaningful, cost-clearing margin on the *held-out* event set → the market-data-only approach is a dead end for that module; pivot to coordination-signal monitoring or shelve it.
- If calibrated probabilities are unreliable (poor Brier/reliability) → don't ship probability-gated alerts; fall back to a conservative, low-recall confluence rule.
- If per-regime event counts are too small for stable calibration → widen the event definition or lengthen history before trusting any per-regime number.

## Caveats
- **Evidence tiering is the whole point:** OFI/order-book imbalance, volatility clustering, BTC→alt lead-lag, and P&D coordination-signal detection are *empirically supported*; OI/funding/liquidation/on-chain flows are *plausible-mechanistic context*; SMC/ICT and standalone CVD-divergence are *largely folklore* for prediction. Each is flagged inline.
- **Direction vs magnitude:** the two strongest patterns (volatility expansion, and to a degree volume/OI anomalies) predict *that* a move/vol-expansion is likely, **not its direction**. Any directional claim needs a separate, independently justified input.
- **Latency reality:** the best-validated micro-signals (OFI/L2) decay in seconds–minutes and are **not manually executable**; they are context/automation-only. Your overnight horizon is more forgiving but pushes you toward *slower* structural signals whose evidence is weaker.
- **Transfer risk:** most P&D and microstructure evidence comes from spot microcaps or equities/FX; transfer to liquid Binance USD-M perps is partial and unproven. Where crypto-perp-specific evidence is absent, that is stated.
- **Data quality:** OI is systematically misquoted on some venues; aggregated CVD/funding feeds are inconsistent; low-liquidity symbols carry wash-trading contamination. Prefer Binance-native, point-in-time data.
- **All numeric thresholds** (volume-z cutoffs, ATR multiples, funding %, R-multiples, "bottom-5% band width," 55–60% breakout accuracy) are **starting heuristics from heterogeneous sources requiring your own calibration**, not validated constants.
- **No investment advice / no guarantees:** nothing here is personalized investment advice or a claim of guaranteed results. The realistic goal is a calibrated, EV-positive precision uplift over base rate — not reliable prediction of specific moves.
- Several practitioner figures (SMC win rates, vendor breakout stats, some funding thresholds) come from non-peer-reviewed sources and are labeled as such; treat them skeptically. Victor & Hagemann's 85%/99%/0.995 figures were read from secondary sources (the IEEE full text is paywalled) and should be verified against the original if used in production planning.
